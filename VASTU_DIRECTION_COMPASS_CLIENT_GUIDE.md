## What Is Vastu Shastra?

Vastu Shastra (वास्तु शास्त्र) is an ancient Indian science of architecture and spatial arrangement, rooted in the Vedas. The word *Vastu* means "dwelling" and *Shastra* means "knowledge" or "science." At its core, Vastu is about aligning your built environment with the natural forces around you — sunlight, wind, the earth's magnetic field, and the five elements (Pancha Bhuta).

Unlike superstition, many Vastu principles have a practical basis:
- **East-facing entrances** capture the beneficial morning sun
- **North orientation** aligns with the earth's magnetic axis, which Vastu links to wealth flow
- **South-West bedrooms** sit in the heaviest, most structurally sound zone of the building
- **South-East kitchens** follow the path of the sun and wind for natural ventilation near fire

Vastu is not about tearing down walls. It is about making informed decisions during design and construction — and applying simple, non-structural adjustments when a layout is already fixed.

## General Vastu Remedies (No Demolition Required)

When a room cannot be moved, these general principles help balance the energy. These are not prescriptive — think of them as supportive adjustments.

### Entrance Issues
- Hang an **Om symbol, Swastika, or auspicious torana** above the door
- Place a **Tulsi (Holy Basil) plant** near the entrance — it purifies air and energy
- Ensure the entrance is **well-lit, clean, and clutter-free** at all times
- Add a **Kolam or Rangoli** threshold design (the geometric patterns naturally guide energy flow)

### Kitchen Issues
- **Face East while cooking** — this alone corrects much of the imbalance
- Place a **copper vessel** near the stove area
- Use **red, orange, or yellow** accents in the kitchen to strengthen fire energy
- Keep the gas stove and water source (sink) **on opposite sides** of the kitchen (fire and water should not face each other)

### Bedroom Issues
- **Sleep with your head facing South or West** — never North (disrupts the body's magnetic alignment)
- Avoid **mirrors facing the bed** — they are said to disturb sleep and amplify anxiety
- Use **earthy tones** (ochre, beige, brown, terracotta) in the master bedroom
- Remove **electronics and work equipment** from the bedroom if possible

### Bathroom Issues
- Keep the **toilet lid closed** at all times when not in use
- Place a **small plant** (money plant, pothos) near the bathroom window
- Use **white or light grey tiles** — dark bathrooms accumulate heavy energy
- Ensure **strong ventilation** — this is both Vastu-correct and practically essential

### Pooja Room Issues
- If you cannot have a dedicated prayer room, create a **small altar in the NE corner** of your living room or any room
- The **deity idols should face West** (so the worshipper faces East) or South (worshipper faces North)
- Keep the space **absolutely clean** — no clutter, shoes, or unrelated items near the altar
- Use **ghee lamps or natural beeswax candles** rather than electric lights where possible

### General Principles for Any Room
- **NE corner** — always keep light, open, and clean. Never place heavy furniture, storage, or clutter here
- **SW corner** — always keep heavy. Place the heaviest furniture, storage units, and the master bed here
- **Light and ventilation** — East and North walls should ideally have more windows than South and West
- **Colours** — use lighter, cooler tones (blues, greens, whites) in the North and East zones; warmer, earthier tones (ochre, terracotta, brown) in the South and West zones
- **Clutter** — energetically the biggest Vastu defect of all. A clean, organised home is more Vastu-compliant than a perfectly aligned but cluttered one

## Five Elements & How They Relate to Your Home

Vastu is built on the *Pancha Bhuta* — the five great elements. Every direction, room, and material connects to one or more of these.

| Element | Direction | Quality | Expressed In |
|---------|-----------|---------|--------------|
| **Earth (Prithvi)** | SW, S | Weight, stability, permanence | Heavy walls, bedrooms, storage |
| **Water (Jala)** | N, NE | Flow, clarity, wealth | Water bodies, blue/white colours, open spaces |
| **Fire (Agni)** | SE | Energy, transformation | Kitchen, electrical, lighting |
| **Air (Vayu)** | NW, E, W | Movement, freshness, growth | Windows, ventilation, study rooms |
| **Space (Akasha)** | NE, Centre | Consciousness, expansion | Open courtyards, the Brahmasthan (centre of the plot) |

The **Brahmasthan** — the very centre of the plot or building — is associated with Space (Akasha) and is governed by Lord Brahma. Classical Vastu strongly recommends keeping the centre of any building open, unbuilt, or at minimum free of heavy structural load. A central courtyard (*Nadumittam*) is the ideal expression of this.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I need to demolish my house if it doesn't score 100?**
No. The vast majority of Vastu corrections are non-structural — orientation of furniture, use of colour, placement of plants and mirrors, and small ritual practices. Structural changes are only ever suggested when planning a new build or major renovation.

**Q: Which direction is the best for the main door?**
North, North-East, and East are the three most auspicious. Among these, many classical texts rank North-East as the single best direction because of its association with Ishanya (Shiva) and the sacred Ishan Kona energy.

**Q: My house faces South — is that bad?**
Not necessarily. A South-facing house is workable with the right internal layout. The entrance should ideally not be at the exact South midpoint — a slight shift toward South-East or South-West is generally considered better. Internal rooms should compensate by following the ideal placement guide above.

**Q: The tool says my kitchen is in the wrong direction. What do I do?**
First, apply the non-structural remedies: face East while cooking, use red/orange accents, keep a copper vessel, and ensure fire (stove) and water (sink) are on opposite sides. If you're renovating, consider relocating the cooking platform toward the SE corner of the existing kitchen space.

**Q: Can I have a prayer space in my bedroom?**
Yes, but place the altar/idol shelf in the **North-East corner** of the bedroom specifically, and keep it elevated. Avoid placing idols directly facing the bed. Keep the altar space clean and separated from the sleeping area.

**Q: What is the Brahmasthan and why does it matter?**
The Brahmasthan is the central zone of your plot or home. Vastu considers it the energy hub — like the eye of a wheel from which all spokes radiate. Heavy construction, pillars, toilets, or staircases at the exact centre are considered highly problematic. Keep this area open, airy, or use it for a central courtyard, skylight, or simply a light, open hallway.

**Q: Does Vastu apply to apartments?**
Yes, at two levels. First, the orientation of the entire building (which direction the building faces). Second, the internal layout of your specific flat. The compass tool works at the flat level — assign rooms based on which direction they sit within your own apartment.

## How Karrcholai Uses Vastu in Design

At Karrcholai, Vastu principles are integrated into our design process from the first site analysis:

1. **Site orientation study** — We analyse the plot's cardinal orientation, the position of true North, and the slope and surrounding context before drawing a single line
2. **Entry and zoning** — Entrance, service areas, and primary living spaces are positioned in their ideal Vastu zones during the brief stage
3. **Structure and mass** — We place the heaviest structural mass in the South and South-West; keep the North and East lighter and more open
4. **Brahmasthan respect** — We design around the central zone, preferring central courtyards, double-height voids, or open circulation cores
5. **Material and light** — Window placement follows Vastu logic: more openings on the North and East, fewer on the South and West
6. **Manaiyadi integration** — Room dimensions are checked against traditional Manaiyadi Sastram calculations (see our Dimension Calculator tool) for complete auspiciousness
